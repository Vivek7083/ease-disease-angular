02 — The ember gradient

One gradient, three sanctioned places

A gradient earns its keep by being rare. This one runs warm-red to warm-orange across a short angle, so it reads as one colour with depth rather than two colours blending — and it drifts almost imperceptibly, on a 26-second loop.

06 — Motion

Motion follows the story, not the scroll

You described the scroll as curiosity → answer → solution → trust. So each beat gets its own kind of movement, and the movement means the same thing every time it appears: scattering is confusion, drawing is connection, ticking is verification, counting is proof.

Beat 1 · Curiosity

Scatter

Symptom words arrive unaligned and out of rhythm — bloating, hairfall, 3am waking — mirroring how they actually turn up in a life.

stagger 90ms · random ±14px offset · 500ms fade-rise

Beat 2 · The answer

Draw

The scattered words snap into a ring and sage lines draw outward from digestion. The signature moment — the thesis, animated.

scroll-scrubbed · stroke-dashoffset · reader sets the pace

Beat 3 · Solution

Progress

Programme steps reveal in sequence with a connecting line running between them — same drawing grammar as the diagram, so it reads as the same idea continued.

sequential reveal · 120ms stagger · gradient rail fills

Beat 4 · Trust

Verify

Ticks draw themselves, lab numbers count up once. Nothing else on the page counts or ticks — so these movements only ever mean "this is checked."

tick 420ms · count 900ms ease-out · fires once

  • 01Scroll-scrubbed, never autoplay. The reader sets the pace of the one big moment. Autoplay on a health site reads as a sales video.
  • 02Nothing moves after it arrives. Once content has landed it stays still — the only permanent motion on the page is the 26-second gradient drift.
  • 03One signature moment per page. The hub diagram on the homepage; on a condition page, that budget goes to that condition's own branch lighting up instead.
  • 04Under 16px of travel, 400–700ms, one easing curve. cubic-bezier(.16,.84,.44,1) everywhere, so the whole site feels like one hand made it.
  • 05Everything is readable with motion off. Reduced-motion users get the finished diagram, ticked ticks and final numbers — never an empty page waiting for an observer.

07 — Two calls I made for you

Where I used my judgement

You left this to me · blue

Blue is in — but only as evidence, never as brand

Your team's board had vivid blue and navy. Dropping #00AEEF into a warm ivory palette at any real size would fight everything around it. But a blue tick next to a claim is doing something no other colour in this system can: it says "this bit is checked" in a language people already read fluently. So blue survives at exactly the size it appears in your reference screenshot — ticks, lab markers, the physician credential — deepened to #0E86C4 so it holds contrast on ivory. It never becomes a button, a heading or a background. If you'd rather stay pure warm, removing it costs one token.

You left this open · gradient placement

Ember does the CTA and the seams — terracotta stays solid everywhere else

You said use the gradient where a gradient makes sense, like section transitions. It does two jobs here: the 3px seam between every band, which is what turns the two-tone base into a rhythm rather than a colour change, and the primary CTA fill, following your reference. Solid terracotta handles everything smaller — icons, underlines, tags — because a gradient under 40px just reads as a muddy orange. Ochre is gone from Sienna & Sage; honey replaces it with one narrow job as a text highlighter, which is what killed the jewellery read.
